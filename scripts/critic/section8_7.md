
### 8.7 §7.1 opened after 8.1–8.6 were written — the attack

*(Sequence is provable: §8.1–8.6 were delivered and appended before §7.1 was
unfolded. Where I agree, the differences below are what distinguish independent
arrival from anchoring.)*

**H1 (execution matrix) — same instrument, missing the property that makes it
converge.** My 8.1 is also a run-everything matrix, so this is agreement — but
H1's surface list is *recalled* ("every seam the demo traverses"), and recalled
lists are the failure being fixed: the lane that recalls seams is the lane that
missed `_run_member_pipeline` and the fold. H1's own list proves it — it omits
`phase_1.py:177`, `submind_arbiter.py:227`, and the non-grounding
`pipeline_runner` executor, three surfaces a 10-line grep returns. Without
tier-1's mechanical derivation + sentinel, the matrix converges once and then
leaks again on the next new caller. H1 also has no mutation tier and no
declared-vs-emitted tier: gaps 3 and 5 (things that redden nothing) and 4 and 6
(vocabulary declared but unemittable) are invisible to a matrix whose cells only
print graph contents.

**H2 (build the renderer next, as the gap detector) — the benefit is real and
available cheaper; the ordering is wrong.** Probe D's record is the argument
*for* throwaway renders per surface (8.4 item 4, and `dr_dump.py` is already
next in the order as the raw instrument). But committing *the* renderer against
graphs known to be wrong in two places (fold grounds nothing; per-exposure
identity unresolved — 8.5 Q6) means the renderer gets shaped by the gaps and
then G2/G6 get shaped by the renderer — the plan's own standing risk ("a guard
authored after the renderer will pass and mean nothing") in mirror image. And
RULES §11's origin story is a throwaway renderer presented as capability;
making an early renderer the *instrument* invites the repeat. Split it: dump
and per-surface renders now, the product renderer after fold + dump land.

**H3 (pre-filter as dev loop, batch 2–3 fixes per gate) — the loop yes, the
batching only conditionally.** The container loop is 8.1's cost model. But
batching breaks the one control this lane has never lost — nine gates predicted
exactly, name-diff attributable to one change. Batch mechanical same-class
fixes; anything touching a quantified claim keeps one-claim-one-gate. And the
pre-filter is structurally blind to FalkorDB round-trips and to the §7
CLI-collection check — a dev loop that never leaves the container goes blind
exactly where probe C stopped.

**H4 (enumerated surface list, examined/unexamined marks) — direction agreed,
mechanism too weak.** A marked list is a to-do list. "Examined" as a persistent
state goes stale the ship after it is written; the member path *had* been
examined — before #153 existed. 8.3 replaces the mark with an event: rows
re-run when their claim or surface is touched, full matrix at milestones, and
the sentinel makes the list self-updating rather than author-maintained.

**Net:** H1+H4 and 8.1/8.3 converge on the instrument; the critic's additions
are the closure sentinel, the mutation and declared-vs-emitted tiers, and
re-run semantics — each traceable to a §4 gap H1/H4 would have missed. H2 is
rejected as ordering, kept as instrument. H3 is kept as loop, cut down as
process.
